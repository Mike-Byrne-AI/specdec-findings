How to read these pages

The three records
sideband asked whether the stream of memory addresses that weight serving hardware sees for free could help a speculative decoder guess better. amber asked whether a draft model's corrections make a byte exact archive of a larger model's text. lockstep built a pure integer coder, which amber carries as an option, and proposed the container such an archive would need.
Speculative decoding
A small draft model proposes several tokens at once; the large target model checks them in one pass. Accepted tokens are emitted, the first rejected one is replaced by a draw from the target's own corrected distribution, so the output is distributed exactly as the target alone would produce. A better guesser buys speed and never changes what can come out.
A finding (F-N)
One numbered entry in a record's findings log, written when the result landed and never rewritten afterwards. A correction is added beside it with a date. In sideband, each finding names the conditions it was measured under and the committed files its numbers come from.
A kill criterion (K-N)
A way the idea could fail, written down before the data existed, and where a size of effect applied, that size fixed before the run it governed. FIRED means the record met that failure; "did not fire" means it did not; UNTESTED means no run ever tested it.
The free baseline
Everything the decoder already knows at the moment it drafts: the target's own next token probabilities, its top candidates and its internal state at the last verified position. A signal in the stream only counts if it adds to that. The first reads used a partial version of this baseline; the deciding read used the full one.
Intervals
Every value drawn with a line through it is a point estimate with a 95% bootstrap interval. The bootstrap resamples whole prompts, not tokens, so the many positions inside one prompt are not counted as independent evidence.
S1 and S3
The two places the stream was read. S1: on a dense Llama model, the slots of the units each tapped layer activated most strongly, which needs the activation values to rank them, so it is not a pure address stream. S3: on a mixture of experts model (OLMoE), which experts each token was routed to, a pure address stream with no values in it.
L1, L2, L3
The three label spaces the probes predict. L1 is sixteen classes, fifteen coarse concept buckets and one glue class for function words; L2 is about five hundred finer concepts plus the glue class; L3 groups the fine buckets into sets that stay coherent across transitions, two of which hold most of the concepts, which is why the record calls L3 a lopsided lens.
Status words
The record's conclusion labels, in capitals: ABSORBED, SURVIVES, NOT-SHOWN, SPLIT and the rest. sideband's conclusion labels were pinned before the runs they govern, with the exceptions the record names; the rest describe what a later entry did. The glossary gives the record's definition; the plain words beside each use on this page are the page's own.
Chips
The small dotted boxes are provenance chips: the file, the line and the first twelve hex characters of that file's sha256 at the tag. Click one to open those lines in the repository at the release tag. The last section of the front page shows how to check a number against the repository.
The pages
The front page holds the verdicts, the record's own opening, the kill board, the charts, the three parts, where the work sits among prior work, and how to check a number. The findings, the errata, the limits, the modules and the glossary each have a page of their own; the bar pinned to the top of every page moves between them. Everything is also on one page, for searching and printing, linked from the foot of every page.

The plain words explanations, the section intros and the reading guide are this page's own wording, not the record's; they add no claim and carry no chip.

The question and the answer, in the record's words

The two paragraphs that open the repository's README at tag v1.0.0, quoted as written: what the three records asked, and what they found. Everything on these pages unfolds from them. The numbers they state are the record's own and carry its chips; the verdicts in the tiles above are this page's wording.

When a large language model generates text, a small draft model can propose several tokens at once and the large model, the verifier, checks them in one pass; the verifier's rejection rule keeps the output exactly its own, so a better draft buys speed and never changes a result. Something also has to fetch the large model's weights, and whatever serves them, a memory device or a tier holding a mixture-of-experts model's experts, sees which weight groups or experts each token touched, as a byproduct, without reading an activation value or a logit. sideband, the central experiment, asked whether that address stream tells the draft model anything the decoder does not already have, and whether it converts into more emitted tokens. amber asked whether the draft model's rank corrections toward target-generated text make a byte-exact archive of that text. lockstep built a pure-integer coder with conformance vectors, which amber carries as an option, and proposed the model-pinned container such an archive would need.

The answers are negative or narrow. sideband tested two streams, S1, a value-aware tap on a Llama-3.1-8B target, and S3, the pure address stream of an OLMoE model's routers (both described in the next section). Its probes do find a small signal: at the pinned L1 cells, with a linear probe, the stream raises top-1 accuracy at predicting the concept bucket of the token two positions ahead by +0.0141 at S1 (the replication harvest) and +0.0147 / +0.0173 at S3 (primary / replication) over a summary of what the decoder already knows (F-15, F-17). Against the fuller free baseline the record then built (the logged summaries of the target's next-token distribution, its hashed top-32 token ids and its anchor hidden state, as this pipeline encodes them), read by the registered reader mlp256, the stream's own margin at those L1 cells is about +0.003 (F-24's words), under the +0.005 floor set in advance, and no material amount more than a scrambled block of the same size adds; at L2 the same reading holds (F-25, read under a rider amendment the record calls "a post-hoc reroute around a fired kill condition"); three stronger probe readers, run later at both sources, did not overturn the L1 result (F-28 at S3, where they also read the L3 lenses; F-29 at S1), and L3, the grouped label space, stays open at both. At S1 two registered attempts to convert the signal into emitted tokens per verify call were null against the bar set in advance (F-19, F-21), and the hidden state its reconstruction lane targeted is an exact function of the target's own next-token distribution, free information (F-23); conversion and reconstruction were never run at S3. amber's codec decodes its archives byte-exactly under an fp32 contract on one box (200 of 200 on the GPU, 24 of 24 on the CPU), and its errata pass corrected 16 of the 21 claims it checked. lockstep's byte-model coder and its conformance vectors stand; the model-backed profile was never built, and the economics behind it did not survive measurement.

Seven ways it could die, written before the data

Before the data, seven ways the idea could fail were written down, and where a size of effect applied, that size was fixed before the run it governed. Two fired: K-2, the stream adds no material amount beyond what the decoder already knows at the registered reader, with one grouped label space left open, and K-3, the signal did not convert into more emitted tokens per check. K-1 did not fire; K-7 did not fire as written, while its gate health diagnostic fired and was routed to an open question. K-4, K-5 and K-6 were never tested: no decoder width sweep ran on real telemetry, nothing in the record is a benchmark harness, and the premise's economics were never reached because both conversion attempts were null. Claims: a steering vector from verifier hidden states at the last verified position, added to the drafter's MLP hidden states, yields +35% accepted tokens / +22% throughput on Llama-3.1-8B + Llama-3.2-1B. DATED CORRECTION (2026-09-04, from the arXiv HTML full text and the public code, read for the OQ-16 Stage-A freeze): the "+35% / +22%" are the paper's headline MAXIMA across model pairs ("up to 35% under standard sampling and 22% under greedy"), not the Llama-3.1-8B + Llama-3.2-1B numbers. Table 1 for our pair — REGIME: draft length k = 8 (blocks of 9), batched decoding (batch 12), <= 128 output tokens, mean of three seeds at T = 1, UltraChat held-out validation as the reported column, unquantized models (no 4-bit anywhere in the paper; training in bf16) — block efficiency tau (accepted+1 per block) pretrained 4.44 -> distilled 4.58 -> SD^2 4.79 under sampling (T=1; speedup 1.07), and 4.51 -> 4.89 -> 5.04 under greedy (speedup 1.11) — i.e. +0.35 / +0.53 tokens per block over the pretrained drafter AT k = 8. Our lane runs gamma = 4 (blocks of 5, ceiling 5 < their 9), NF4 target, batch 1, 256 tokens: the reachable fraction of a k = 8 gain at k = 4 is unmeasured, so their number is a cross-gamma prior, not a bar for ours. Mechanism, from the code (src/models/llama.py, src/model.py, main.py): g_t = W_hml[h_t, m_t, l_t] from verifier layers 3, L/2, L-2 at the last verified position (W_hml initialized as a sum of identities, then LayerNorm); a zero-initialized linear map from g_t to one d_ff-dim bias PER drafter layer ("merged": added to the up-projection output before the SiLU gate product, every layer); refreshed after every verify; loss KL (code default TVD) to the verifier on verifier-generated synthetic text with random block offsets; the drafter is FULLY fine-tuned alongside ("critical to the performance improvement", their Fig. 6 ablation, frozen-drafter steering is weaker); 1xA100-80GB, 6 epochs UltraChat + 1 ShareGPT.
